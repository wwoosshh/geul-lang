The old lighthouse at the edge of the harbor had not guided a ship in many years, but the people of the town still walked out to it every evening. Some went to watch the sun go down over the water, and others went simply because their parents and grandparents had done the same. The path was narrow and covered with small stones, and in the spring it was lined with wild flowers that nobody had planted.

One autumn a young engineer arrived from the city with a plan to turn the lighthouse into a weather station. She carried a box of instruments, a notebook full of drawings, and a letter from the regional office that nobody in the town had expected. The mayor read the letter twice, folded it carefully, and asked her to explain the plan at the next public meeting.

At the meeting she spoke about wind speed, air pressure, and the way storms form far out at sea. She said that a small station at the end of the harbor could warn the fishing boats an hour or two earlier than the radio forecasts did. A retired captain in the front row asked whether the old lamp would be removed. She answered that the lamp would stay exactly where it was, and that the new instruments would fit on the railing outside the lantern room.

The work took most of the winter. Every morning she climbed the narrow stairs with a cup of tea and a bag of tools, and every afternoon a few children waited at the bottom to ask what she had measured that day. She taught them how to read the barometer, how to record the temperature at the same hour each day, and why a sudden drop in pressure usually means that the weather is about to change.

By the time the flowers returned to the path, the station was sending data to the regional office every ten minutes. The fishing boats received their first early warning in the middle of April, when a line of storms moved in from the west faster than anyone had predicted. Every boat was back in the harbor before the first strong gust reached the shore.

That evening more people than usual walked out to the lighthouse. The old lamp was dark, as it had been for years, but the small green light on the new instrument box blinked steadily in the wind, and the children explained to their parents what each number on the screen meant.
